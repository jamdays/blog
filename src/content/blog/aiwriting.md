So, we all read the title. It's not ragebait. I'm just trying to be objective. Really, there is one reason that I've been thinking about this, and it has to do with a past or future blog. Titled right now as "Stylometry". The thumbnail is equally temporary, but right now, it is a picture of south park's emoji analysis.

If you know about either of these things already, or just know latin roots, you should be able to guess where I'm going. I'll start with emoji analysis. The season containing the emoji analysis episodes was about finding the internet troll based on the emojis they use, quitting twitter, and 'member berries. Stylometry is "the study of linguistic style" according to Wikipedia.

For the brief 2 days that I took linguistics, I also remember something about the term "idolect". And I don't need to be those who study linguistics to know that my idolect could almost uniquely identify me. I know few who say diddy, blud, or a combination of both as much as I do. That's not including trickster, those who whatever, and I don't know what else. That's just what I've noticed. Now, as I write this, it is sort of meta, because I am thinking about my own writing style while writing, probably giving me a different linguistic style.

Anyway, although I trust you guys to have concluded where I am going, I'll explain myself anyway. First, the obvious, this information, your choice of words, the length of your sentences, the grammar structures you use, the typos you make and more uniquely identifies you. Even if it doesn't completely, this combined with trivial metadata, like the general time you posted something, or ... definitely does. 

All together, it provides a convincing argument to write with chat gpt. The more you write, the easier it is to see what is unique about what you write, and you are slowly giving up your likeness to be used anywhere, just like deep fakes. Would you sacrifice your likeness for a linkedin post? Would you sacrifice your likeness for a job? 

Why not just write things with AI? Doesn't the content of your message matter more than the linguistic fingerprint you leave behind? What would the point be to keeping your linguistic style private? 

I'm not sure myself how to answer these questions. Maybe I could sacrifice a bit of my likeness for a job I would be satisfied at, something that would fulfill me, and maybe a couple of genuinely written linkedin posts get me there. I'm not a serial killer or ransomer, I don't need to hide my style and stitch together my words from magazine articles.  

There doesn't seem to be major risks in putting my linguistic likeness on the table, and to truly identify someone, you should ask them personal questions only they would know. And the answers should be based on the content not the linguistic style.

More than this though, I've struggled to explain myself because I was defaulting to my regular linguistic style, something you don't realize in the moment, especially if you context switch with little overhead. So maybe, chat gpt would've helped. But, it doesn't actually matter how much chat helps for me, because I learned pretty standard english for the whites. If I need to I can explain myself easily. But maybe for those with different lives than me, their style is less acceptable to the general public. 
If so, why not just generate your text with chat? That way you can be judged on content instead of percieved ease of reading.
